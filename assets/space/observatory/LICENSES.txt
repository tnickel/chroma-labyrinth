Credits are retained. No NASA, ESA, CSA, STScI, JPL or SSI endorsement is implied.

Files are distributed photographic image products, not AI-generated imagery.
The NASA CDN supplies requested maximum dimensions without cropping. No local
recolouring, retouching or invented planetary features were added. GPU texture
compression, scene tone mapping and fade-in/out affect the displayed image.
Planetary photographs are composited over the existing catalogue star field:
dark background pixels reveal the stars, and image edges feather to transparent.
The ring light contribution is preserved separately from background occultation.
Elliptical mattes fitted to the photographic limbs keep the dark planetary bodies
opaque. Jupiter's photograph crops the unlit hemisphere; its matte extends beyond
that crop as plain black, without adding photographic surface detail. This is
an illustrative silhouette estimate, not measured shape/topography. The Carina
image retains its full rectangular presentation. Original JPEGs are unchanged.
Aspect ratios are preserved; these remain archive projections, not real flybys.

The surrounding sky uses the existing NASA SVS Deep Star Maps 2020 asset:
https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/4851/
Credit: NASA's Scientific Visualization Studio / Ernie Wright.
It is a catalogue-derived visualization, not one all-sky photograph.
See ../SOURCE.json for its original asset and conversion provenance.

The observatory is a fictional exhibition during the compressed Mars transfer.
Jupiter and Saturn are not physical flybys along that trajectory. Image
directions, image scale and duration are exhibition choices, not an astronomical
ephemeris. Planetary distances/timing in the flight are not a launch solution.
