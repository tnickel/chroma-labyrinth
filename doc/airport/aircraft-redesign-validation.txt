Window pressure skin: all four corners of all 18 window fields receive three
inward rays at distinct insets (216 rays). The test intersects only the actual
ContinuousWindowPressureSkin mesh and verifies its material is opaque. Interior
walls, seats, window reveals and other geometry cannot make a missing skin
triangle pass. Nine additional rays through each actual window aperture (162
rays) must remain clear against this same mesh, preventing a repair that simply
fills the whole field.

Cockpit bulkhead: all unique vertices and triangle centroids of the actual
CurvedFlightDeckBulkhead (194 points) are compared against intersections with the
actual VaultedCabinRoof triangles. Each sample must have roof cover and remain
under that rendered roof envelope. The check does not reuse the producer's
ellipse formula and also detects a displaced bulkhead or exposed shoulders.

The current Exterior count is therefore 1202; the earlier 626-check results
above are retained as historical runs, not relabelled with the new test count.

Defects detected and resolved during this review
-----------------------------------------------
The first actual geometry run found inconsistent fan blade winding and negative
enclosed volume. The producer corrected the global section-normal orientation.
The remaining ten inconsistently oriented tip-cap edges were traced to a cap
centre on the uncambered chord, outside the thin curved blade profile. The cap
centre now follows the camber centreline. The final manifold/orientation checks
pass for all 37 marked closed volumes.

The initial test's rounded-cell vertex weld falsely separated nearly identical
seam positions at cell boundaries. The test now searches adjacent spatial cells
and checks actual distance <=0.01 mm. No production geometry was changed to hide
those numerical false positives. Degenerate-triangle detection uses a squared
cross-product tolerance of 1e-16, below the producer's 1e-14 skip threshold.

Exterior tests check finite unit normals, clockwise front-face consistency,
outward fuselage normals, declared smooth-seam normals, closed-volume edge
incidence/orientation, positive signed volume, and relevant flight/gate layers.
Panorama and dome tests separately check intended transparent apertures and
real collision routes; open windows are not treated as closed-solid defects.
