CHROMA LUNAR - VALIDATION, 2026-10-30

Environment: Godot 4.7.2.stable.official.ed1daf0bf, Windows x64,
Vulkan 1.4.341, Forward+, NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070, 1920x1080.

Production journey
- 16 phases, 674 seconds / 11:14. Moon approach, 150-second Copernicus
  survey, Earthrise, return to Earth and boarding at the original gate.
- Closest altitude is 5 km above the lunar reference sphere (1737.4 km
  radius), not a terrain-following clearance of exactly 5 km.
- Scenic time compression and staged viewing attitude; no orbital mechanics
  or current astronomical constellation is claimed.

Actual images and heights
- 16k global lunar color; 8k global LOLA height encoding.
- 4096-square WAC image covers roughly 485 km with ~118 m samples.
- Native 1024-square LOLA height crop supplies the surrounding terrain.
- NAC photo and regional terrain: 4951x8192, ~6.84 m samples from 5 m source
  products; fine stereo data only inside its documented narrow footprint.
- Real 8k catalog-derived star map and historical NASA Earth cloud map.
- All nine asset SHA256 checks passed. Sources, transformations and licenses:
  assets/space/SOURCE.json and LICENSES.txt. No runtime network requests.

GPU review

and scenery. The production controller advanced through the whole journey;
the journey clock paused only between screenshot samples. Benchmark windows
ran the normal flight clock, passengers and flight HUD. No concurrent GPU
test ran. Vsync was disabled. Thirteen captures and gate restoration passed.

Visual fixes verified on the GPU
- Grazing lunar rays converge to a solid horizon instead of missing terrain.
- Wide photographic coverage replaces the white coarse ring beyond the NAC
  strip. Exposure matching and geographic feathering blend image products.
- Transparent glass no longer paints diffuse structural shadows across sky
  or lunar terrain. Dielectric reflections and actual window frames remain.
- Distant Earth uses real cloud coverage instead of striped procedural noise;
  optical 10-degree diagnostic confirms readable continents and cloud systems.
- The Earth remains its physical angular size of approximately two degrees
  when viewed near the Moon; the 10-degree capture is explicitly a QA zoom.

Screenshots are unretouched Godot viewport captures. The photo's deep crater
shadows are present in the original low-Sun source. Stars are exposed for
visibility alongside the lunar terrain; display exposure is a scenic choice.
Fine rocks everywhere on the Moon, live weather and scientific navigation
accuracy are outside the source-data resolution and the demo's scope.
